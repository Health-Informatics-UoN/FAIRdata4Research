FAIR Data for Research

A standardised, transparent process for researchers to access NHS data for research across the East Midlands.

About FAIRdata4Research

Across healthcare providers there are significant operational challenges involved in making data available for research. The current process is time consuming, open to error and not easily repeatable. Our ambition is to develop a standardised and transparent process for researchers to access data that can be replicated at other study sites within the East Midlands.

Available Data

Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust (NUH) is the founding Data Collection Centre for FAIRdata4Research. FAIRdata4Research is open to other NHS organisations in the East Midlands to join, and the clinical specialty datasets available are continually expanding.

What if the data I need is not listed?

Please get in touch with the Data Access team at nuhnt.dataaccess@nhs.net. The data you need may still be held by a Data Collection Centre even if it is not yet part of FAIRdata4Research, so it may still be worth applying.

Adding new data takes extra work by the data warehouse teams, so timeframes will be longer than for data that is already available. Requests for new data will also be considered as part of our prioritisation process for deciding which data is added next.

How to Access Data

  1. Get sponsor approval Projects using FAIRdata4Research require a sponsor. This should not be the same level of review as might be expected in other studies, but still required. The sponsor is usually the organisation that employs you and they will have their own forms for you to complete first to generate a protocol. They are likely to also to want to see your completed access request form. The form is online but here is a Word version to use during this stage.
  2. Approval from FAIRdata4Research Data Access Committee. To submit a request to use FAIRdata4Research database, please complete this form: FAIRdata4Research – Data Access Request Form v1, once you have sponsor approval.
  3. All data included in the request is fully justified to ensure only the required data is provided.
  4. No identifiable person-level data can be provided. National Data Opt-Out is applied to ensure those who have opted out of having their data used for research or planning are not included in the dataset. Further data minimisation may include reducing the accuracy of dates by calculating age at events or randomly but consistently shifting the dates.
  5. The researcher must use and provide details of the secure environment they will use and evidence the suitability of that environment, for example, the NHS England Secure Data Environment. Data will not be sent directly to the researcher. The researcher cannot download the row level data.
  6. Contractual agreements in place, including data sharing agreements. Data from each Collection Centre will be sent to the secure environment directly and a processing agreement with the organisation supplying the secure environment put in place. This will also include agreed data deletion and archiving expectations. Onward sharing or use outside of the approved project is not permitted.

Terms of reference for the Data Access Committee are available here: Terms of Reference.

How We Protect Data

Patient data should only ever be used for research in a way that protects confidentiality, keeps data secure and is in the public interest. FAIRdata4Research is built on the FAIR Data Principles and the Five Safes Framework, which is also used by NHS England, Health Data Research UK and the Office for National Statistics.

Data stays where it was created

We do not build a central copy of patient data. Data stays with the NHS organisation where it was collected, under that organisation’s control, for as long as possible. It only leaves once a project has been approved by the Data Access Committee, and only to an approved secure environment. Our work with patients and the public showed this is the approach they prefer.

Three stages of protection

  1. Direct care data. Identifiable data is handled only by each hospital’s existing data warehouse team, who already have access as part of the direct care team. They prepare the data for research as follows:
    • NHS numbers are replaced with a pseudonymised ID. Only the data warehouse team holds the link between the two.
    • All other identifiers, including hospital numbers, are removed.
    • Dates of birth are changed to the first of the month.
    • Postcodes are replaced with a wider statistical area (Lower Layer Super Output Area).
    • The National Data Opt-Out is applied. Anyone who has opted out is not included.
  2. Research view. The pseudonymised data is held separately from identifiable data, and still within each hospital. Researchers can check whether we hold the data they need using the HDR UK Cohort Discovery tool. This only ever returns counts of patients and gives no access to the data itself.
  3. Research dataset. Only once a project is approved is data prepared for it:
    • Only the data that is justified for the project is provided.
    • Data is further minimised, for example by converting dates to ages at events or shifting dates consistently.
    • A new pseudonymised ID is created for each project, so data cannot be linked across projects.
    • The opt-out is checked again for every new extract.

Secure environments only

Data is never sent to researchers directly. It goes straight from each hospital to an approved secure environment, such as an NHS England Secure Data Environment. Any other environment must show it meets similar standards. The environment must:

  • prevent researchers from downloading row-level data;
  • check everything entering or leaving the environment (an ‘air lock’);
  • check that research outputs match what was approved and could not identify anyone.

A data processing agreement is put in place with the organisation providing the environment, which includes agreed data deletion and archiving. Onward sharing or use outside the approved project is not permitted.

Independent review of every request

Every application is assessed by the Data Access Committee against the Five Safes: safe projects, safe people, safe settings, safe data and safe outputs. The committee is co-chaired by a lay member and includes patient and public representatives alongside clinical, research, governance and technical leads. Each hospital decides whether its own data is released. Researchers must be named individuals, sign an agreement on how they will use the data and complete training. See the Terms of Reference for full details.

Oversight and transparency

  • The Access Committee reports to the FAIRdata4Research Management Committee, which is accountable to the NUH Research and Innovation Quality and Assurance Committee.
  • The database has a favourable ethical opinion (IRAS 349333), with Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust as sponsor.
  • Each hospital keeps a record of all data released and the project it was released for.
  • Approved projects are registered in the HDR UK Data Use Register, and a lay summary of every approved request is published.
  • Approved projects must report their outcomes and impact every year.
